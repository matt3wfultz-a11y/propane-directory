Refill vs Exchange: Why Refilling Saves Money

If you've ever wondered why you should bother finding a refill station instead of just swapping at the hardware store, this table explains it clearly:

OptionGas You Actually GetTypical PriceCost Per Gallon
Refill (local dealer or U-Haul)Full 20 lbs / ~4.7 gal$16–$22$3.40–$4.70
Exchange (Blue Rhino / AmeriGas cage)Only ~15 lbs / ~3.5 gal$24–$28$4.80–$5.60

Exchange programs intentionally fill tanks to only 15 lbs — leaving 25% of your cylinder empty. You pay more and get less. On a single 20-lb tank, refilling saves you $7–$12 per fill. Over a full summer grilling season with four or five fills, that's $35–$60 back in your pocket.

The only time exchange makes sense: your tank is expired and can't be legally refilled, you need gas immediately at 10 PM, or you're miles from any refill station. Otherwise, refill every time.

What to Bring to a Propane Refill Station

  • Your cylinder — any size; transport it upright, ideally in a truck bed or with windows cracked if carrying inside a vehicle
  • Check the collar date — the date stamped on the collar (e.g., "04 14" = April 2014) must be within 12 years, or recertification is required. Dealers won't fill expired tanks.
  • OPD valve — your cylinder must have an Overfill Prevention Device valve (triangular handwheel) to be refilled legally
  • Payment — most dealers accept cash and card; a few smaller dealers are cash-only

If your tank is expired, see our guide on propane tank recertification to understand your options.
