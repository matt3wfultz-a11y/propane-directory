Propane Refill Stations in El Paso, TX: 2026 Guide to Locations & Prices

August 28, 2026

A propane refill is the most cost-effective way to keep your cylinders full, and El Paso has multiple refill stations spread across the city. Whether you have a 20-lb grill tank, a 33-lb forklift cylinder, or a 100-lb residential tank, local dealers and service points can fill it for less than you'd pay at an exchange cage. Here's everything you need to know before making the trip.

How Propane Refill Works

Unlike an exchange program — where you trade your tank for a pre-filled one — a refill fills your own cylinder at a dispenser. The process takes only a few minutes:

  1. Bring your cylinder to the refill station. Most El Paso locations accept walk-ins; no appointment needed.
  2. A technician inspects the tank for damage, checks the date stamp on the collar (expired tanks cannot be filled), and verifies the OPD valve is functioning.
  3. The cylinder is placed on a scale and filled by weight to its rated capacity — a 20-lb tank receives up to 20 lbs of propane, not the 15 lbs that exchange programs provide.
  4. You pay based on the volume filled (price per gallon × gallons dispensed) and transport the tank home upright.

The whole visit typically takes 5–10 minutes at a dedicated dealer, slightly longer at a service station that offers refill as a secondary service.

Where to Find Propane Refill Stations in El Paso

Refill locations are spread across the El Paso metro, from the Westside to the Lower Valley:

  • Dedicated propane dealers: Local and national companies (Ferrellgas, independent dealers) offer the lowest per-gallon prices and can fill all cylinder sizes. Look for locations on the east side along Montana Avenue, northeast El Paso near Dyer Street, and the Lower Valley.
  • U-Haul locations: Multiple El Paso U-Haul centers offer propane refill by weight. A reliable option for standard grill cylinders when you're already in a retail area.
  • Tractor Supply: El Paso-area stores offer outdoor refill service with competitive pricing and extended weekend hours.
  • RV parks and dealerships along I-10: Useful if you're traveling through and need a larger tank filled quickly.

What to Check Before You Go

  • Date stamp on the collar: A stamp reading "06 12" means June 2012 — the tank needed recertification by June 2024. Dealers cannot legally fill an expired cylinder. Some El Paso dealers offer on-site recertification; others can direct you to a nearby testing station.
  • OPD valve: Cylinders made before 1998 may lack an Overfill Protection Device valve. Most El Paso dealers will not fill tanks without one.
  • Visible damage: Rust, dents, or damage to the valve will cause a dealer to refuse the fill for safety reasons. A tank in that condition needs to be inspected or replaced.
  • Transport: Always transport a filled cylinder upright and secured. In a car, crack the windows; in a truck bed, strap the tank to prevent rolling.
