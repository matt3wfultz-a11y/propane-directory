Propane Tanks Near Me in El Paso, TX: Where to Buy, Refill & Exchange

May 15, 2026

Searching for propane tanks near El Paso? Whether you need to buy a new cylinder, refill an existing one, or swap at an exchange cage, El Paso has plenty of options across every part of the city. This guide covers where to find tanks, what each service costs in 2026, and which option saves you the most money.

What "Propane Tanks Near Me" Actually Means

When people search for propane tanks near El Paso, they're usually looking for one of three things:

  • Buy a new empty cylinder — You need a tank you don't own yet. Hardware stores, big-box retailers, and propane dealers all sell empty cylinders.
  • Refill an existing tank — You already own a cylinder and just need it filled. Refill stations top off your tank by weight at the lowest per-gallon rate available.
  • Exchange a tank — Swap your empty cylinder for a pre-filled one at a self-serve cage. Convenient but costs more and delivers less gas (tanks are only filled to 15 lbs, not 20).

El Paso has all three. The right choice depends on what you already have and how much you value convenience vs. cost.

Where to Buy a New Propane Tank in El Paso

If you need a cylinder from scratch, here's where to find them near El Paso:

  • Home Depot and Lowe's: Stock 20-lb empty cylinders ($35–$45) in the outdoor or garden section. Also sell pre-filled tanks via the exchange cage outside.
  • Walmart: Sells new 20-lb cylinders in sporting goods or outdoors for $35–$50.
  • Tractor Supply Co.: Carries a wider range — 20-lb, 33-lb, and 40-lb cylinders — useful for RV, generator, or forklift applications.
  • Local propane dealers: Dealers like West Texas Propane, DAE Propane, and AmeriGas bulk locations sell new cylinders in all sizes and can fill them on the spot. Often the best deal when you need both the tank and the gas.

When to Choose Exchange Instead

Exchange makes sense in a few specific situations:

  • Your tank is expired: Propane cylinders expire 12 years after the manufacture date stamped on the collar. Many refill dealers won't fill an out-of-date tank. Exchange programs will accept it and swap it for a newer cylinder — effectively getting you a free tank upgrade.
  • After hours or on weekends: Exchange cages at Walmart, Home Depot, and El Paso gas stations are self-serve and available 24/7. Refill dealers usually close by 5–6 PM and many don't operate Sundays.
  • You ran out mid-cookout: No appointment, no wait — if convenience is the priority, exchange delivers.

How to Check If Your Tank Is Still Good

Before driving to a refill station, check the date stamp on your cylinder's collar ring. It looks like "06 14" — that means the tank was manufactured in June 2014. Tanks must be recertified after 12 years, so a 2014 cylinder was due in 2026. If your tank is expired, an exchange swap is the easiest path to a new cylinder.
