What to Check Before Buying a Used or Reconditioned Tank

Some propane dealers and online marketplaces sell reconditioned cylinders at a discount. Before you buy:

  • Check the date stamp: Look on the collar ring. A stamp like "06 14" means June 2014. Cylinders must be recertified 12 years after manufacture — so a 2014 tank is due in 2026.
  • Inspect the OPD valve: All tanks made after 1998 should have a triangular handwheel (OPD = Overfill Prevention Device). If the valve is round, the tank is too old to be legally filled by most dealers.
  • Check for rust and dents: Surface rust on the bottom is common; deep pitting or dents near the valve are dealbreakers — dealers will refuse to fill them.
  • Verify the collar is intact: The collar protects the valve. A missing or badly bent collar is a safety issue.

Tank Sizes for Specific Uses Near El Paso

Choosing the right size saves money and prevents running out mid-use. Here's what works for the most common El Paso propane uses:

  • Backyard grilling: A standard 20-lb cylinder lasts 18–20 hours of cooking time on a typical gas grill. For a family that grills a few times per week during the El Paso summer season, expect 1–2 fills per month.
  • Patio heaters: A 20-lb tank lasts 8–10 hours on a typical 40,000 BTU patio heater — enough for several cool West Texas evenings before refilling.
  • RV travel: Most RVs use 20-lb or 30-lb tanks. If you're heading out through the Chihuahuan Desert, fill up in El Paso before leaving — refill stations thin out between here and the next major city.
  • Residential heating backup: A 100-lb tank provides a useful buffer for a space heater or fireplace insert during the occasional El Paso cold snap. For whole-home backup, a 250-gallon or 500-gallon tank with bulk delivery makes more sense.
