Whether you need a brand-new propane cylinder, want to exchange an empty tank at a retail cage, or are looking to refill the one you already own, El Paso has you covered. This guide explains exactly where to find propane tanks near El Paso — and how much you should expect to pay in 2026.

Where to Find Propane Tanks Near El Paso

El Paso has three distinct ways to get a propane tank, depending on whether you need to buy, exchange, or refill:

  • Buy a new tank: Hardware stores like Home Depot, Ace Hardware, and Tractor Supply locations throughout El Paso sell 20-lb cylinders off the shelf, typically priced $35–$50 for the empty tank. You'll need to get it filled separately at a refill station.
  • Exchange an empty tank: Blue Rhino and AmeriGas exchange cages are found outside most Walmart, Lowe's, and grocery store locations across El Paso. Hand in your old tank and receive a pre-filled one — no appointment needed.
  • Refill your existing tank: Local propane dealers, U-Haul locations, and Tractor Supply stores offer by-weight refill service. This is almost always cheaper than exchange per gallon of gas received.
  • Larger residential tanks: For 100-lb cylinders and above, dedicated propane dealers can supply, place, and service tanks for residential or commercial use. This typically involves a delivery arrangement rather than a drop-in transaction.

Checking Your Propane Tank Before You Go

Before heading to a refill station near El Paso, check a few things on your cylinder:

  • Date stamp on the collar: The manufacture date is stamped on the metal collar (e.g., "06 14" = June 2014). Tanks expire 12 years from manufacture — after that, a dealer may refuse to fill it. Look for a recertification stamp (an "E" followed by a date) if the tank has been re-qualified.
  • Visible damage: Dents, rust spots, or bent valve handles are grounds for rejection. If in doubt, exchange the tank rather than attempting to refill it.
  • OPD valve: Tanks manufactured after 1998 are required to have an Overfill Protection Device valve (a triangle-shaped handwheel). Older tanks without one cannot be legally refilled.

Transporting Propane Tanks Safely in El Paso

Texas and federal regulations require propane cylinders to be transported upright and secured. A few practical notes for El Paso drivers:

  • Always keep the cylinder valve closed and capped during transport
  • Stand the tank upright in a truck bed or vehicle trunk — never lay it on its side
  • Crack a window if transporting inside a car or SUV to prevent pressure buildup
  • Never leave a filled cylinder in a parked vehicle on a hot El Paso summer day — temperatures inside cars can exceed 120°F, which creates a pressure risk
