Your Three Options for Propane Tanks Near El Paso

Option 1: Buy a New Propane Tank Near El Paso

If you need a new cylinder, the easiest places to buy one in El Paso are:

  • Home Depot and Lowe's — Stock standard 20-lb grill cylinders (empty) for $30–$50. You'll then need to fill or exchange it.
  • Walmart — Carries 1-lb camping cylinders and standard 20-lb tanks, sometimes pre-filled through the exchange program.
  • Sporting goods stores (Academy Sports, Bass Pro) — Good for camping-size cylinders (1 lb, 16.4 oz).
  • Local propane dealers — Some sell recertified used tanks at a discount. A good option if you need a 33-lb, 40-lb, or 100-lb cylinder that big-box stores don't carry.

Option 2: Refill Your Propane Tank Near El Paso

Refilling at a local dealer is the most cost-effective way to get propane in El Paso. You pay only for the gas you receive, measured by the gallon, and your tank is filled to its full rated capacity.

Where to Refill Near El Paso

  • Dedicated propane dealers — Ferrellgas, AmeriGas, and independent local suppliers operate bulk fill stations that can handle any cylinder size. These offer the lowest per-gallon rates in El Paso.
  • U-Haul locations — Multiple El Paso U-Haul centers fill 20-lb and 33-lb cylinders by weight. Reliable and competitively priced.
  • Tractor Supply — Outdoor propane fill stations staffed during store hours. Good for standard cylinder sizes.
  • RV and outdoor dealers — Particularly useful if you're near Interstate 10 or Fort Bliss and need larger tanks filled.

Option 3: Propane Tank Exchange Near El Paso

Exchange cages — the orange Blue Rhino and blue AmeriGas units — are parked outside most El Paso big-box stores and many gas stations. You hand in your empty tank, pay at the register, and walk out with a filled one.

Exchange is fast, but it costs significantly more than a dealer refill:

Exchange tanks are filled to only 15 lbs — 25% below the rated 20-lb capacity — while costing $7–$12 more than a dealer refill. Over a full grilling season with five fills, that's $35–$60 extra for no additional gas.

When exchange makes sense: when your cylinder is expired or damaged and you need a replacement tank, or when you urgently need propane on a weekend evening and no refill station is open.

Before You Go: Tank Safety Checks

Whether you're refilling or exchanging, check these things before you head out:

  • Certification date: Look at the collar of your cylinder for the manufacture or recertification date stamp (e.g., "06 14" means June 2014). Tanks more than 12 years old require recertification before a dealer will fill them. An expired tank is fine to use at an exchange cage — the exchange company takes it and gives you a compliant one.
  • Valve and OPD: Modern cylinders have an OPD (overfill protection device) valve — a triangular handle. Tanks without an OPD (very old cylinders) may be refused at refill stations.
  • Physical condition: Look for rust, dents, or visible damage. A corroded or deeply dented tank should be retired, not refilled.
  • Transport: Always transport propane cylinders upright. In a car or SUV, crack a window; in a truck, use the bed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I buy a propane tank near El Paso?

New 20-lb cylinders are available at Home Depot, Lowe's, and Walmart for $30–$50 empty. Larger cylinders (33 lb, 40 lb, 100 lb) are best purchased from local propane dealers, some of whom also carry recertified used tanks at lower prices.

How much does it cost to refill a propane tank in El Paso?

A 20-lb grill cylinder costs $16–$21 to refill at a local dealer in El Paso ($3.40–$4.50 per gallon). Larger tanks scale proportionally. Exchange cages cost $24–$28 for a 15-lb fill — more expensive per gallon and less gas.

Is refill or exchange better for propane tanks in El Paso?

Refill wins on cost almost every time. You get a full 20-lb fill for $16–$21 at a dealer, versus a 15-lb partial fill for $24–$28 at an exchange cage. The only exception is when your tank is expired or damaged — exchange lets you get a replacement cylinder at the same time.

What size propane tank do I need?

A standard 20-lb cylinder runs a typical gas grill for 18–20 hours of cook time. For patio heaters, larger grills, or extended camping trips, a 33-lb or 40-lb cylinder offers more capacity without the weight of a 100-lb tank.
