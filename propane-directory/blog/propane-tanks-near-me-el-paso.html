Should You Buy New, Refill, or Exchange?

The right choice depends on your situation:

  • Buy new if you don't already own a cylinder. Pick up an empty 20-lb tank at a hardware store for $25–$45, then take it to a dealer or U-Haul for a refill. You'll own the tank outright and pay lower refill rates going forward.
  • Refill if you already have a cylinder that's within its service life (under 12 years old, or recertified). This is the best long-term value — El Paso refill prices run $3.40–$4.50 per gallon, which is below the national average.
  • Exchange if you need gas right now, your tank is too old to refill, or a dealer isn't convenient. The exchange cage at the hardware store doesn't require any wait — just swap and go. The trade-off is a higher cost and you only get 15 lbs instead of a full 20-lb fill.

Check Your Tank Before You Go

Before heading to a dealer or exchange cage, take 30 seconds to check your cylinder:

  • Expiration date: Look at the collar (the round ring at the top of the tank). You'll see a date stamped like "06 14" — that means June 2014. Tanks are valid for 12 years from manufacture, so that tank expired June 2026. Dealers won't fill an expired cylinder. If yours is past the 12-year mark, you can get it recertified at many propane dealers, or simply exchange it at a cage — exchange programs accept expired tanks.
  • OPD valve: Tanks manufactured after September 1998 are required to have an Overfill Prevention Device (OPD) valve, identifiable by the triangular handwheel. Older tanks without OPD valves generally can't be legally refilled.
  • Physical condition: Dents, rust, or damaged valves are grounds for a dealer to refuse a fill. Inspect the cylinder before you make the trip.
