Propane Refill in El Paso, TX: Stations, Prices & How It Works

October 30, 2026

A propane refill is the most cost-effective way to keep your cylinder topped off — and in El Paso, TX, refill stations are plentiful. From dedicated propane dealers to U-Haul centers and hardware stores, you can get a tank filled fast without paying the premium that exchange programs charge. Here's everything you need to know before your next refill.

Why Refill Beats Exchange in El Paso

The exchange cages at Walmart, Home Depot, and H-E-B are tempting because they're fast. But the economics strongly favor refilling your own cylinder:

OptionGas You ReceiveTypical CostCost Per Gallon
Refill (local El Paso dealer)~4.7 gal (full 20-lb fill)$16–$21$3.40–$4.50
Exchange (Blue Rhino / AmeriGas)~3.5 gal (15-lb fill only)$24–$28$4.80–$5.50

Exchange programs fill tanks to only 15 lbs — not the full 20-lb capacity. That means you pay more per gallon and get 25% less gas. On a single tank, the refill saves you $7–$12. Over a grilling season, that easily adds up to $40–$60 or more.

Propane Refill Stations in El Paso

El Paso has refill stations spread across the metro area. Here's where to look:

Dedicated Propane Dealers

Local and national dealers — Ferrellgas, Suburban Propane, and independent propane companies — offer the lowest per-gallon prices. They fill tanks of all sizes, from standard 20-lb grill cylinders to 100-lb residential tanks. Some also rent or sell new cylinders if you don't own one yet.

U-Haul Locations

Several U-Haul centers across El Paso (Westside, Northeast, and near Fort Bliss) offer propane refill by weight. They handle standard 20-lb and 33-lb cylinders and are a convenient stop if you're already near a U-Haul location. Call ahead to confirm the fill station is staffed and operational.

Tractor Supply

Select Tractor Supply stores in the El Paso area have outdoor propane fill stations. Great if you're in the area for other supplies — just pull up to the fill island at the store entrance.

How a Propane Refill Works: Step by Step

  1. Bring your cylinder. Any size is fine — the station fills by weight. Bring it empty or partially full; you only pay for the gas added.
  2. The technician inspects the tank. They'll check the date stamp, valve condition, and look for damage. Tanks more than 12 years old need recertification first.
  3. The tank is weighed, filled, and re-weighed. Filling by weight ensures accuracy — you pay for exactly what you receive, charged by the pound or gallon depending on the dealer.
  4. Pay and go. Most El Paso dealers accept cash and card. The process typically takes under 10 minutes.

Before You Go: Tank Checklist

  • Check the collar date stamp. Format is MM YY (e.g., "06 14" = June 2014 manufacture). Tanks over 12 years old may be due for recertification — see our recertification guide.
  • Inspect for visible rust, dents, or a damaged valve. A station will reject a compromised tank.
  • Transport upright — in a truck bed, or in the passenger compartment with windows cracked. Never in a sealed trunk.
  • Bring payment. Most El Paso stations take card and cash.
