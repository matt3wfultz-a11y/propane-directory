Propane Refill in El Paso, TX: Cost Guide, Best Stations & Tips

October 2, 2026

Propane refill is the most cost-effective way to keep your grill, heater, or generator fueled in El Paso. Unlike the exchange cages you see outside retailers, a propane refill fills your cylinder to full capacity — and at a significantly lower price per gallon. This guide explains how propane refill works, what you'll pay at El Paso stations in 2026, and how to get the most out of every fill.

How Propane Refill Works

A propane refill station fills your existing cylinder directly, charging by the gallon or by weight. The process takes about five minutes:

  1. Pull up to the fill station or dealer with your cylinder (empty or partially full).
  2. A technician weighs your empty cylinder (the tare weight is stamped on the collar) to determine how much propane it can hold.
  3. Propane is pumped into the cylinder by liquid weight until the tank is full — typically ~4.7 gallons (20 lbs) for a standard grill cylinder.
  4. The tank is reweighed to verify the fill, and you pay per gallon or per pound.

The whole visit usually takes under 10 minutes. No appointment needed at most El Paso locations.

What to Check Before Getting a Propane Refill

  • Date stamp on the collar: Propane cylinders expire 12 years after manufacture. The stamp is on the neck ring below the valve — "06 14" means June 2014, so it expired June 2026. Most dealers won't fill an expired cylinder. See our guide on propane tank recertification for your options.
  • Valve condition: OPD (Overfill Prevention Device) valves are required on all cylinders manufactured since 1998. The valve handle will have three lobes. If your older cylinder has a handwheel valve (round wheel), dealers may decline to fill it.
  • Physical condition: Dents, rust, or damage to the cylinder body or foot ring can disqualify a tank from being filled. Inspect it before making the trip.
  • Tare weight: The tare weight (TW) stamped on the collar tells the dealer how much your empty cylinder weighs, so they know how full it is after filling. It should be clearly visible.

How to Tell When Your Tank Needs a Refill

The most reliable method: weigh the cylinder. Subtract the tare weight (TW stamped on the collar) from the current weight — the difference is how many pounds of propane remain. A 20-lb cylinder at 15 lbs total weight with a 17-lb TW has just 0 lbs of propane left (i.e., it's essentially empty). See our full guide on how to tell if a propane tank is empty.
