The Eastside and Far East El Paso have several propane refill options, particularly along Montana Avenue and US-54. U-Haul locations in this corridor refill 20-lb and 33-lb cylinders by weight. Local propane dealers serving the Eastside also offer larger tank fills for agricultural and residential customers in the valley east of Loop 375.

The Westside — including the Mesa Hills and Kern Place corridors — has propane refill service available at hardware retailers and some U-Haul locations near I-10. Tractor Supply locations near the Westside serve the suburban and semi-rural fringe toward the New Mexico border.

The Northeast — including areas around Dyer Street, Hondo Pass, and the Fort Bliss corridor — is well-served for propane. How a Propane Refill Works: Step by Step

If you've never used a propane refill station before, the process is straightforward. Here's exactly what to expect at most El Paso locations:

  1. Drive up with your tank secured upright — in a truck bed, or in the trunk or back seat with windows cracked. Never transport a propane cylinder in an enclosed car trunk on a hot day.
  2. An attendant (or self-serve kiosk) checks your tank — they'll inspect the valve, OPD stamp, and collar date. If your tank is more than 12 years old, they may decline to fill it. A date stamp of "04 14" means April 2014, making the tank recertification-due as of April 2026.
  3. The tank is weighed empty — the tare weight (TW) is stamped on the collar. The attendant fills to TW + the tank's rated capacity (e.g., TW 17.2 lbs + 20 lbs = 37.2 lbs total).
  4. You pay by the gallon or as a flat fill — most El Paso dealers charge per gallon. A full 20-lb fill is approximately 4.7 gallons.
  5. The valve is checked and the tank is handed back — reattach it to your grill or appliance with the valve closed, then open slowly when ready to use.

Total time at a busy station: 5–10 minutes. At a slow station or self-serve kiosk: under 5 minutes.

5 Tips to Save Money on Propane Refills in El Paso

  1. Refill, don't exchange. Exchange cages are everywhere, but they charge a premium — $24–$28 for only 15 lbs of gas. A local refill fills your 20-lb tank to the full 20 lbs for $16–$21. You get more gas and spend less every time.
  2. Call ahead for the current per-gallon rate. Propane prices move with wholesale markets. Calling two or three local dealers takes 2 minutes and can reveal a $0.30–$0.50/gal spread — worth it on a large tank.
  3. Don't wait until the tank is completely empty. You'll pay the same per gallon whether you refill at 20% or at empty — but running completely dry risks air and moisture entering the valve, which requires bleeding before the next fill (some dealers charge extra for this).
  4. Fill in summer, not before a holiday weekend. Propane demand spikes around Memorial Day, Fourth of July, and Labor Day in El Paso. Refilling the week before avoids any temporary price bumps and station lines.
  5. Check Blue Rhino's coupon page before an exchange. If you genuinely need the convenience of an exchange cage, Blue Rhino frequently posts $3–$5 off coupons online. Worth a 30-second check before paying full price at the cage.

When to Recertify Instead of Refill

Most El Paso refill stations will refuse to fill a cylinder that is past its recertification date. The date stamp on the collar tells you when the tank was manufactured — a stamp like "08 13" means August 2013. Propane cylinders must be recertified 12 years after manufacture (August 2025 in that example) and again every 5 years after that.

If your tank is expired, you have three options: recertify it through a qualified propane dealer (usually $10–$20), exchange it at a cage (the cage programs accept expired tanks and give you a newer cylinder), or buy a new cylinder.
