Propane Gas Near Me in El Paso, TX: Refill, Exchange & Bulk Delivery

July 3, 2026

If you need propane gas near El Paso, TX, you have several good options depending on how much gas you need and how you use it. El Paso is well-supplied — the city sits on a major propane distribution corridor along the Texas-New Mexico border, which keeps local prices competitive and supply reliable. This guide breaks down every way to get propane gas in El Paso, with 2026 pricing for each.

Three Ways to Get Propane Gas Near El Paso

Most El Paso residents get propane through one of three channels:

  1. Cylinder refill at a local dealer or station: You bring your tank — 20 lb, 33 lb, 40 lb, or larger — to a refill station and pay by the gallon. This is the cheapest option and fills your tank to its full legal capacity. Local suppliers like West Texas Propane, Bob's Propane, DAE Propane, and U-Haul offer this service across the El Paso metro.
  2. Tank exchange at a retail cage: Blue Rhino and AmeriGas exchange cages are located outside most Walmart, Home Depot, and grocery store locations in El Paso. You drop off an empty tank and take a pre-filled one. It's fast, but exchange cages only fill tanks to 15 lbs — 25% less gas than a full refill — and the per-gallon cost runs higher.
  3. Bulk propane delivery to a home tank: If you heat your home with propane, run a whole-home generator, or have a large tank (250, 500, or 1,000 gallons), suppliers like Ferrellgas and AmeriGas offer scheduled delivery or automatic top-off service. Per-gallon prices for bulk are lower than cylinder pricing, but you typically need a minimum delivery amount.

Propane Gas Uses in El Paso

Propane is used across El Paso for a wide range of applications, and the right supplier depends on your use case:

  • Outdoor cooking and grilling: Standard 20-lb cylinders, refilled at local dealers or U-Haul. Refill beats exchange by a wide margin in cost.
  • Patio and outdoor heating: Tall patio heater cylinders (20–40 lb) are available at most propane dealers in El Paso. El Paso's mild winters mean patio heaters see heavy use from October through March.
  • RV and camping: 20-lb, 30-lb (ASME), and 33-lb cylinders are commonly filled at dealers along I-10 and near Fort Bliss. See also our guide on propane for RVs and camping.
  • Home heating and water heating: Some El Paso homes not on the natural gas grid use propane for space heating, water heaters, or dual-fuel systems. Bulk delivery from suppliers like Ferrellgas is typically the most cost-effective path.
  • Backup generators: Propane generators are popular in the El Paso area due to occasional grid instability during extreme heat events. A 100-lb cylinder or a 250-gallon home tank both support generator use.
  • Commercial and food service: Restaurants, food trucks, and catering operations in El Paso typically use large cylinders (100 lb) or arrange regular delivery from commercial suppliers.

Where to Get Propane Gas by Area in El Paso

El Paso's propane suppliers are spread across the metro area. A few general patterns:

  • East El Paso / Northeast: AmeriGas exchange cages at major retailers; U-Haul locations offer refill service.
  • West El Paso / Westside: Multiple hardware and home improvement stores with exchange cages; independent dealers serve this area as well.
  • Lower Valley (Socorro, Fabens corridor): Fewer big-box retail options; local independent dealers and farm supply stores often fill this gap. Check the Socorro and Fabens directory pages.
  • Far East / Horizon City: Growing residential area with both exchange cages and at least one local refill option. See the Horizon City directory.
  • Canutillo / Upper Valley: Limited retail options; independent propane dealers serve agricultural and residential customers. See the Canutillo directory.

Propane Gas vs. Natural Gas in El Paso

Most of El Paso's residential neighborhoods are served by El Paso Electric and El Paso Natural Gas, so many homes already have natural gas lines. If you have a choice between the two fuels, there are real cost differences. Propane delivers more BTUs per cubic foot than natural gas but typically costs more per BTU at retail cylinder prices.
